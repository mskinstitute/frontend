# Advanced SmartArt & Charts

## Data Visualization: Dynamic Charts & Advanced SmartArt

Numbers buried in dense paragraphs often go unnoticed by executives. Embedding dynamic charts (Bar, Line, Pie, Waterfall, and Scatter plots) brings numerical trends and comparative metrics to life directly within your document.

### 1. Inserting a Chart in Microsoft Word

1. Navigate to **Insert** tab > **Illustrations** group > click **Chart**.
2. Select your target chart geometry:
   - **Column / Bar**: Comparing discrete categories (e.g. quarterly sales across regions).
   - **Line**: Showing continuous performance trends over time (e.g. monthly revenue).
   - **Pie / Doughnut**: Portraying percentage shares of a whole 100% total.
   - **Hierarchy (Treemap / Sunburst)**: Visualizing proportional category breakdowns.
   - **Waterfall / Combo**: Financial balance variances and multi-metric comparisons.
3. Click **OK**.
4. Word embeds the chart and automatically opens a small **embedded Excel worksheet window** (`Chart in Microsoft Word`).
5. Replace the dummy sample values with your real business metrics; the chart on your canvas updates dynamically in real time!
6. Close the Excel window when finished.

### 2. Linking Live External Excel Charts (Auto-Updating)

If your finance team updates an Excel model every week, you don't want to re-create the chart in Word repeatedly:
1. In Microsoft Excel, select your finished chart and press **Ctrl + C** (Copy).
2. Switch to your Word document canvas.
3. On the **Home** tab, click the downward arrow under **Paste** > select **Keep Source Formatting & Link Data** (or *Use Destination Theme & Link Data*).
4. *Result*: The Word document establishes an active OLE link to the external Excel file!
5. Whenever the Excel file's data changes, right-click the chart in Word and click **Update Link**; the chart in your report refreshes instantly!

### 3. Chart Elements & Formatting

When a chart is selected, use the three floating buttons on its top-right corner:
- **Chart Elements (+)**: Toggle Axis Titles, Data Labels, Data Table, Error Bars, Trendlines, and Legends.
- **Chart Styles (Brush)**: Apply designer color themes and dark/light background styling.
- **Chart Filters (Funnel)**: Instantly show or hide specific data series or categories without touching the Excel data sheet!

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. What happens when you insert a new Chart into a Microsoft Word document?
A. Word crashes
B. A compact embedded Excel worksheet opens to let you input numbers
C. Word converts the page to PowerPoint
D. The document is saved as a picture
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Word opens a paired embedded Excel spreadsheet window to manage the chart's underlying data points.
---

### 2. Which paste option establishes an active link between an Excel chart and your Word report so edits in Excel sync automatically?
A. Paste as Picture
B. Keep Source Formatting & Link Data
C. Paste as Unformatted Text
D. Paste Special > Bitmap
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Keep Source Formatting & Link Data" creates an active OLE link that syncs updates from the parent workbook.
---

### 3. Which floating icon next to a chart allows you to toggle Axis Titles, Data Labels, and Legends?
A. The Filter Funnel
B. The Chart Elements Plus sign (+)
C. The Paint Brush
D. The Layout Options icon
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The plus icon (+) opens the Chart Elements checklist to toggle axes, labels, and legends.
---

### 4. Which chart category is best suited for demonstrating percentages of a single 100% total market share?
A. Line Chart
B. Pie / Doughnut Chart
C. Scatter Plot
D. Radar Chart
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pie and Doughnut charts are designed specifically to display proportional parts of a whole.
---

### 5. Under which Ribbon tab is the Insert Chart tool located?
A. Home
B. Insert
C. Design
D. Data
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Insert > Illustrations houses the Chart, SmartArt, and Shapes tools.
