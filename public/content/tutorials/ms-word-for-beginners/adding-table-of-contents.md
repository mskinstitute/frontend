# Adding Table of Contents

## Automated Table of Contents (TOC)

Typing a Table of Contents manually with dots and page numbers is frustrating and immediately breaks whenever paragraphs shift. Microsoft Word can generate and maintain an automated, interactive Table of Contents in seconds.

### The Golden Rule: Apply Heading Styles First!

Word builds the Table of Contents by scanning your document for text formatted with built-in heading styles:
- `Heading 1` (`Ctrl + Alt + 1`) ➔ Top-level Chapter title (TOC Level 1)
- `Heading 2` (`Ctrl + Alt + 2`) ➔ Major Section heading (TOC Level 2)
- `Heading 3` (`Ctrl + Alt + 3`) ➔ Sub-section topic (TOC Level 3)

If you format headings manually with bold and big fonts without applying heading styles, Word will report: *"No table of contents entries found"*.

### Generating the Table of Contents

1. Place your cursor on the blank page where you want the TOC to appear (typically right after the Title/Cover page).
2. Navigate to **References** tab > click **Table of Contents** on the far left.
3. Choose **Automatic Table 1** or **Automatic Table 2**.
4. Word scans the document and renders:
   - Every heading indented by its structural level.
   - Tab leader dots (`.........`).
   - Exact corresponding page numbers.
   - Interactive hyperlinks: Clicking any heading while holding **Ctrl** jumps directly to that section!

### Updating the Table of Contents (`F9`)

Whenever you add new chapters, delete paragraphs, or page numbers shift:
1. Click anywhere inside the Table of Contents box.
2. Click the **Update Table...** button at the top (or press the **F9** function key).
3. Choose:
   - **Update page numbers only**: Super-fast; keeps existing headings and only refreshes page numbers.
   - **Update entire table**: Scans for new, deleted, or renamed headings and reconstructs the full list.

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. What must be applied to titles in your document for Word to build an automatic Table of Contents?
A. Bold and Yellow Highlight
B. Built-in Heading Styles (Heading 1, Heading 2, etc.)
C. Double Spacing
D. Bullet points
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Word scans for Heading styles (Heading 1, Heading 2, etc.) to compile the Table of Contents.
---

### 2. What function key updates the selected Table of Contents to reflect recent document changes?
A. F5
B. F7
C. F9
D. F12
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Pressing the F9 key refreshes Word dynamic fields, including the Table of Contents.
---

### 3. How can a reader navigate directly to a specific chapter from the Table of Contents on screen?
A. Hold Shift and click the heading
B. Hold Ctrl and click the heading
C. Double-click the page number
D. Press Alt + Enter
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Ctrl + Click on any TOC heading follows the internal hyperlink directly to that section.
---

### 4. Which update option should you select if you added brand new sections or changed heading titles?
A. Update page numbers only
B. Update entire table
C. Restart numbering
D. Rebuild Styles
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Update entire table" refreshes the TOC hierarchy, adding new titles and removing deleted headings.
---

### 5. Under which Ribbon tab is the Table of Contents gallery found?
A. Insert
B. Layout
C. References
D. Review
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Table of Contents command is the primary feature of the References tab.
