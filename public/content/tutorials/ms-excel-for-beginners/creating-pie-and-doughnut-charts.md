# Creating Pie and Doughnut Charts

Pie and doughnut charts illustrate proportional composition—how individual component slices contribute to a 100% total whole. When used correctly for market share, budget splits, or survey responses, they deliver immediate visual clarity.

---

## The Anatomy of Pie and Doughnut Charts

- **Pie Chart**: A circular graphic divided into wedge slices proportional to each category's percentage of the total.
- **Doughnut Chart**: A modern variation with a hollow circular center. The hollow center can house key headline metrics (e.g., placing a large "$12.4M Total" summary KPI card inside the ring!).

---

## Strict Rules for Effective Pie Charts

Because human eyes struggle to compare subtle differences in triangular 2D angles, data visualization experts enforce three fundamental rules:

1. **The 5-to-7 Slice Rule**: Never create a pie chart with more than **5 to 7 slices**. A pie chart with 25 slices creates an illegible, rainbow-colored mess. Combine smaller long-tail categories into a single "Other" slice!
2. **Sum to 100%**: Pie charts must represent complete parts of a single whole. Never use a pie chart for survey data where participants could check multiple checkboxes!
3. **Always Add Data Labels**: Slices look identical without percentages. Always click the chart, click the green **'+' (Chart Elements)** button, check **Data Labels**, and format them to display **Category Name & Percentage**.

---

## Step-by-Step: Building a Professional Pie Chart

1. Select your 2-column data range (e.g., Column A: Expense Category, Column B: Amount).
2. Go to **Insert > Charts > Insert Pie or Doughnut Chart > 2D Pie**.
3. Right-click any slice and choose **Format Data Series**:
   - **Pie Explosion**: Pulls all slices outward from the center for stylistic emphasis.
   - **Angle of First Slice**: Rotates the pie so the primary or largest slice begins at the top 12 o'clock position.
   - **Exploding a Single Slice**: Double-click just *one* specific slice (e.g., your company's product slice) and drag it outward by 10% to create a dramatic focal point!

---

## Converting to a Modern Doughnut Chart

1. Right-click the pie chart and select **Change Chart Type...**
2. Choose **Doughnut**.
3. In Format Data Series, adjust the **Doughnut Hole Size slider** (default is 75%; 55%–65% often looks more refined).
4. Insert a text box inside the hollow center to display the total aggregate budget!

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. What is the primary data visualization purpose of a Pie Chart?
A. To track continuous time-series fluctuations over 10 years
B. To show how individual component parts contribute proportionally to a 100% whole
C. To correlate two independent numeric variables on an X-Y plane
D. To list raw inventory records
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Pie charts represent part-to-whole proportions, where all slices collectively sum to 100% of the aggregate total.

---

### 2. What is the recommended maximum number of slices for a legible, professional pie chart?
A. 2 slices
B. 5 to 7 slices
C. Exactly 20 slices
D. Unlimited
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Limiting pie charts to 5 to 7 slices prevents visual crowding and keeps angle proportions distinguishable to viewers.

---

### 3. How can you emphasize or isolate just one specific slice in an Excel pie chart?
A. Delete all other slices
B. Double-click the individual slice and drag it slightly outward (Slice Explosion)
C. Change the font to bold
D. Rotate the monitor
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Double-clicking an individual slice selects only that segment, allowing you to pull it outward in an explosion effect for visual emphasis.

---

### 4. What advantage does a Doughnut Chart offer over a traditional solid Pie Chart?
A. It holds 1,000 times more data
B. Its hollow center can display summary metric labels (e.g., Grand Total) and it occupies less visual weight
C. It prints in 3D automatically
D. It calculates formulas faster
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Doughnut charts offer a contemporary look and free up central canvas space for key summary KPI metrics.

---

### 5. Why must data labels displaying percentages be added to pie charts?
A. Because Excel cannot save pie charts without labels
B. Because human eyes have difficulty accurately estimating and comparing subtle 2D slice angles without numerical values
C. To hide the chart title
D. To make the file size smaller
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Visual perception studies prove humans struggle to judge raw angles; explicit percentage labels eliminate ambiguity.
