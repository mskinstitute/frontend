# Reviewing, Comments, Comparing & Inspecting/Protecting Presentations

Presentations in modern enterprises are rarely created by a single person in isolation. Mastering peer review comments, real-time cloud co-authoring, version comparison, metadata sanitization, and presentation security ensures seamless team collaboration and prevents embarrassing data leaks.

---

## Modern Comments & @Mentions

On the **Review** tab (or via the **Comments** button on the top-right):
- **Adding Comments**: Select any text box, chart, or slide and click **New Comment** (or press **`Ctrl + Alt + M`**).
- **Pinning Comments**: In modern PowerPoint, comments can be pinned to specific shapes or objects so colleagues know precisely which graphic the feedback references.
- **@Mentions**: Type `@ColleagueName` inside a comment. PowerPoint automatically sends an email notification with a deep-link directly to that slide, assigning the task.
- **Resolving Comments**: Once edits are complete, click **Resolve** to archive the conversation thread while preserving review audit history.

---

## Real-Time Co-Authoring & Version History

When presentations are saved on OneDrive or SharePoint:
- **Simultaneous Editing**: Multiple team members can work on different slides (or even the same slide) simultaneously without file locking conflicts.
- **Presence Indicators**: Colored avatar badges reveal who is currently viewing or editing specific slides in the thumbnail pane.
- **Version History**: Go to **File > Info > Version History**. You can browse previous revisions across days and weeks, view changes, and restore older drafts if an error occurs.

---

## Comparing Two Presentations (Merge & Diff)

When colleagues email you an edited copy of your presentation instead of using cloud sharing:
1. Open your master presentation.
2. Go to **Review > Compare**.
3. Select the colleague's modified file and click **Merge**.
4. The **Revisions task pane** opens on the right, listing every discrepancy:
   - Added, modified, or deleted text
   - Altered shapes or rearranged slides
5. Check or uncheck individual items to **Accept** or **Reject** changes with complete editorial control!

---

## Document Inspector: Sanitizing Hidden Data Before Sharing

Presentations often contain confidential internal notes, hidden slides, or author metadata that should never reach external clients:
1. Go to **File > Info > Check for Issues > Inspect Document**.
2. Check options to inspect:
   - *Comments and Annotations*
   - *Document Properties and Personal Information*
   - *Off-slide Content* (shapes or drafts dragged off the visible canvas)
   - *Hidden Slides*
   - *Presentation Notes* (private speaker scripts)
3. Click **Inspect**.
4. Review the report and click **Remove All** next to sensitive categories to permanently sanitize the file before sending!

---

## Protecting Your Presentation

Under **File > Info > Protect Presentation**:
- **Always Open Read-Only**: Prompts recipients to open in read-only mode to prevent accidental tampering.
- **Encrypt with Password**: Enforces a strong AES password required to open and decrypt the presentation.
- **Mark as Final**: Disables typing, editing commands, and proofing marks, signalling that the file is the approved final release.

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. How can you notify a specific colleague and assign them a task directly inside a slide comment?
A. Type their full home address
B. Type "@" followed by their name or email address inside the comment box
C. Change the slide background to their favorite color
D. Save the file with their name in the title
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Typing an @mention (e.g., @Jane) in a comment sends an automated email alert with a direct link to the designated slide.

---

### 2. Which feature on the Review tab allows you to compare two different versions of a presentation file and selectively accept or reject edits?
A. Translate
B. Compare
C. Check Accessibility
D. SmartArt
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The "Compare" command analyzes differences between the active presentation and a secondary file, letting presenters accept or reject alterations.

---

### 3. Why is running the "Document Inspector" (File > Info > Check for Issues) considered an essential best practice before emailing decks to external clients?
A. It checks if the computer is connected to the internet
B. It identifies and removes hidden private notes, off-slide draft content, and personal author metadata that could cause security leaks
C. It reduces PowerPoint battery consumption
D. It adds animations to blank slides
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The Document Inspector strips sensitive metadata, hidden speaker notes, off-canvas scratch graphics, and revision logs before external dissemination.

---

### 4. What indicator reveals that another team member is actively working on a specific slide during real-time co-authoring?
A. The computer makes a continuous ringing sound
B. A colored avatar circle with their initials or photo appears next to the slide thumbnail
C. The slide turns black
D. PowerPoint closes automatically
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Visual presence bubbles showing user initials or profile photos indicate who is actively viewing or modifying slides in real-time co-authoring.

---

### 5. What security option under File > Info > Protect Presentation sets the file to read-only status to inform viewers that it is the completed, approved draft?
A. Encrypt with Password
B. Mark as Final
C. Compress Pictures
D. Hide All Slides
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Mark as Final" disables typing and proofing commands while displaying a notification banner that the presentation is the finalized, approved edition.
