With the exponential surge in digital payments, mobile banking, and cloud apps across India, cyber attacks, phishing SMS links, and credential theft are at an all-time high. Cybersecurity is no longer an optional IT concern—it is a mandatory life skill for everyone.

## 1. Always Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Passwords alone are extremely vulnerable to data leaks and dictionary attacks. Protect your Google accounts, banking portals, and WhatsApp with an authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator) or hardware keys rather than SMS OTPs wherever supported.

## 2. Recognize Smishing and Social Engineering

Banks, electricity departments, and government bodies will **never** phone you demanding your ATM PIN, UPI PIN, or CVV. If you receive an SMS urging you to download an APK file or click a link to *"avoid electricity meter disconnection"* or *"update bank KYC"*, block and report the sender immediately.

> **Golden Rule of UPI:** Entering your UPI PIN is **ONLY** required when sending money, **NEVER** when receiving money!

## 3. Keep Software and Operating Systems Updated

Most automated ransomware and zero-day exploits prey on known security holes for which Microsoft, Apple, or Google have already distributed patches. Keeping auto-updates enabled prevents over 90% of automated commodity attacks.

## 4. Use a Dedicated Password Manager

Using the same password across multiple websites means that if one forum gets compromised, hackers gain access to all your accounts. Use an encrypted password manager (such as Bitwarden or 1Password) to generate unique 16+ character passwords.

## 5. Avoid Sensitive Transactions on Open Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi networks in railway stations, cafes, and hotels often lack network isolation. Attackers on the same network can intercept unencrypted traffic or execute Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) spoofing. Always use mobile cellular data or a reliable VPN when accessing banking portals.

## 6. Verify HTTPS and Full Domain Names

Always check your browser address bar. Phishing sites frequently register visually deceptive domains (e.g., `sbl.co.in` instead of `sbi.co.in`). Ensure the connection is encrypted via SSL/TLS.

## 7. Regularly Audit Installed Apps & Permissions

Review which mobile apps have access to your contacts, SMS, microphone, and location. Delete any apps you have not opened in the last 3 months.
