### 1. Understanding Internet of Things (IoT)

The **Internet of Things (IoT)** refers to the vast network of physical objects ("things") that are embedded with sensors, software, actuators, and network connectivity, allowing them to collect, exchange, and act upon data automatically over the internet without human intervention.

- **Origin of Term:** The term *"Internet of Things"* was coined by **Kevin Ashton** in **1999** during a presentation at Procter & Gamble (P&G).

#### Key Components of an IoT System:

1. **Sensors & Actuators:** Sensors detect changes in physical environment (temperature, light, humidity, motion, smoke, GPS location). Actuators perform physical actions (turning on a fan, locking a door, opening a water valve).
2. **Connectivity (Network):** Transmits collected data via Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Cellular (4G/5G), RFID, or Zigbee.
3. **Data Processing (Cloud Platform):** Software processes sensor data in the cloud (e.g., detecting if factory machine temperature exceeded 80°C).
4. **User Interface (Mobile App / Dashboard):** Notifies the user or triggers automatic actions (e.g., sends mobile notification: *"Front door unlocked"*).

#### Real-World Applications of IoT:
- **Smart Homes:** Smart bulb turned on by phone, Alexa voice assistant controlling AC.
- **Precision Agriculture:** Soil moisture sensors trigger drip irrigation pumps only when crops need water, saving electricity and groundwater.
- **Smart Cities:** Automated LED streetlights that brighten when cars approach and dim during empty nights; smart trash bins that alert municipal trucks when full.
- **Healthcare Wearables:** Smartwatches monitoring heart rate, oxygen level (SpO2), and alerting emergency contacts in case of a sudden fall.

### 2. Understanding Big Data Analytics

Every minute, millions of YouTube videos are watched, crores of UPI payments are processed, Google handles billions of searches, and millions of WhatsApp messages are sent. Traditional database software like Microsoft Excel or standard SQL cannot store or process this immense avalanche of information. This vast ocean of information is called **Big Data**.

![Networking & Internet](/images/tutorials/ccc/internet-networking.jpg)

#### The 5 V's of Big Data (Exam Special):

| The 'V' | Meaning & Explanation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Volume (आकार)** | The astronomical scale of data generated—measured in Terabytes (TB), Petabytes (PB), Exabytes (EB), and Zettabytes (ZB). |
| **2. Velocity (गति)** | The unprecedented speed at which new data is generated and streamed in real-time (e.g., stock market trades, live GPS signals). |
| **3. Variety (विविधता)** | The diverse forms of data: Structured (tables, rows/columns), Semi-structured (JSON, XML), and Unstructured (videos, audio, social media photos). |
| **4. Veracity (सत्यता)** | The trustworthiness, accuracy, and quality of the data. Dirty or corrupt data leads to flawed business predictions. |
| **5. Value (मूल्य)** | The ultimate business worth or actionable insights extracted from data analysis. Without business value, data is useless. |

#### Popular Big Data Frameworks:
- **Apache Hadoop:** Open-source framework using distributed storage (HDFS) across clusters of commodity computers.
- **Apache Spark:** High-speed in-memory data processing engine for real-time analytics.

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. Who coined the term 'Internet of Things' (IoT) in the year 1999?
A. Charles Babbage
B. Kevin Ashton
C. Tim Berners-Lee
D. Alan Turing
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Kevin Ashton first coined the term 'Internet of Things' in 1999 while working on RFID technology at Procter & Gamble.
---

### 2. Which of the following is NOT one of the traditional '5 V's' of Big Data?
A. Volume
B. Velocity
C. Variety
D. Vibration
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The recognized 5 V's of Big Data are Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, and Value. Vibration is not a Big Data attribute.
---

### 3. Which component in an IoT system is responsible for converting electrical signals into physical movement or action (such as opening a valve)?
A. Sensor
B. Actuator
C. Monitor
D. Keyboard
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Actuators convert control signals into physical motion (e.g., motor rotations, opening valves, switching relays), whereas sensors measure physical inputs.
---

### 4. Which of the following represents an example of 'Unstructured' data in Big Data?
A. Relational database table with student roll numbers and marks
B. Video files, voice recordings, and social media tweets
C. An Excel worksheet with employee salary columns
D. A CSV file containing bank account numbers
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Videos, audio clips, and freeform text messages do not fit neatly into rows and columns, making them unstructured data (which constitutes over 80% of world data).
---

### 5. Which open-source software framework is widely used for distributed storage and processing of massive Big Data datasets across computer clusters?
A. Apache Hadoop
B. MS Word
C. Adobe Photoshop
D. VLC Media Player
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Apache Hadoop is a popular open-source framework that allows distributed storage (HDFS) and processing of massive datasets across computer clusters.
