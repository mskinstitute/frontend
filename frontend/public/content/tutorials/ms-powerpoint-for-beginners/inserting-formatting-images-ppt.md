# Inserting & Formatting Pictures, Crop, Artistic Effects & Background Removal

Visual imagery creates immediate emotional resonance with your audience. PowerPoint provides powerful built-in photo editing tools—from aspect-ratio cropping and artistic filters to automatic background removal—eliminating the need for external photo editing software.

![Media & Video Tools](/images/tutorials/ms-powerpoint/media-audio-video.svg)

---

## Inserting Images into Slides

You can insert imagery via several routes:
1. **Via Ribbon**: Go to **Insert > Pictures**:
   - **This Device**: Browse high-res photos stored locally on your computer.
   - **Stock Images**: Built-in Microsoft 365 library of millions of curated, royalty-free high-resolution photos, cutout people, icons, and illustrations.
   - **Online Pictures**: Search Bing images with Creative Commons filtering.
2. **Via Content Placeholder**: Click the centered **Pictures** icon inside any content layout placeholder. The image will automatically size itself to fit within the placeholder's boundaries.
3. **Drag-and-Drop**: Drag image files directly from Windows File Explorer onto the slide canvas.

---

## The Picture Format Ribbon Tab

Selecting any image brings up the contextual **Picture Format** tab:
- **Corrections**: Adjust Sharpness/Softness and Brightness/Contrast presets.
- **Color**: Tweak Color Saturation, Color Tone (Warm/Cool temperature), or **Recolor** (e.g., convert photo to monochromatic brand blue or sepia).
- **Artistic Effects**: Apply textured filters such as Pencil Sketch, Watercolor Sponge, Film Grain, or Glass.
- **Picture Styles & Borders**: Apply rounded corners, subtle dropshadows, or modern frames.

---

## Mastering Crop Techniques

Cropping allows you to reframe subjects and fit photos into specific geometric spaces:
- **Standard Crop**: Click **Picture Format > Crop**. Black crop handles appear along the perimeter. Drag inward to trim unwanted areas, then click outside the image or press **Enter**.
- **Crop to Shape**: Click the arrow beneath **Crop > Crop to Shape** and choose any shape (e.g., Circle, Oval, Rounded Rectangle, Hexagon). PowerPoint instantly masks the photo into that vector shape.
- **Aspect Ratio Locking**: Crop to exact proportions such as **16:9** (widescreen), **1:1** (square headshot), or **4:3**.
- **Fill vs. Fit**:
  - **Fill**: Scales image to completely cover the shape boundaries without distorting proportions (excess edges cropped).
  - **Fit**: Scales entire image so all pixels are visible within the bounding box.

## Remove Background Tool (Instant Cutout)

PowerPoint's background removal tool lets you isolate subjects without Photoshop:
1. Select the photo and click **Picture Format > Remove Background**.
2. PowerPoint highlights areas detected as background in bright magenta.
3. Click **Mark Areas to Keep** (green pen) to draw lines over details that were mistakenly flagged for removal.
4. Click **Mark Areas to Remove** (red pen) to eliminate remaining unwanted background fragments.
5. Click **Keep Changes**. The background becomes 100% transparent PNG!

---

## Compressing Pictures for Fast Sharing

High-resolution photos can bloat a `.pptx` file from 2 MB to 150 MB, causing email bounces and laggy slide show playback:
1. Select any picture and click **Picture Format > Compress Pictures**.
2. Choose resolution target:
   - **HD (330 ppi)**: High definition for large 4K displays.
   - **Print (220 ppi)**: Excellent quality for commercial printing.
   - **Web (150 ppi)**: Ideal for web conferencing and presentations.
   - **E-mail (96 ppi)**: Minimizes document size for emailing.
3. Uncheck *Apply only to this picture* to compress every image across the entire presentation in a single click!
4. Check **Delete cropped areas of pictures** to permanently purge hidden cropped pixels.

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. Which tool under the Picture Format tab allows you to mask an image into a circle or rounded rectangle?
A. Remove Background
B. Crop to Shape
C. Artistic Effects
D. Color Tone
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The "Crop to Shape" feature allows you to crop and mask any rectangular photo into geometric shapes like circles, hexagons, or shields.

---

### 2. What color does PowerPoint use to highlight areas marked for deletion when using the Remove Background tool?
A. Cyan
B. Bright Magenta (Pink)
C. Neon Yellow
D. Translucent Green
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** PowerPoint paints all detected background areas in bright magenta/pink, allowing presenters to see exactly which sections will become transparent.

---

### 3. How can you significantly reduce the overall file size of a PowerPoint deck containing dozens of large camera photos?
A. Export each slide to Word
B. Select a picture and use "Compress Pictures" on the Picture Format tab with "Delete cropped areas" enabled
C. Change the slide transition to None
D. Remove all slide numbers
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Compress Pictures" lowers pixel resolution across all images and permanently deletes hidden cropped pixels, drastically reducing the file size.

---

### 4. What is the difference between "Fill" and "Fit" when adjusting a cropped picture in PowerPoint?
A. Fill converts photos to black-and-white, while Fit converts to color
B. Fill sizes the picture to completely cover the container area (cropping excess), while Fit scales it so the entire image fits inside without cropping
C. Fill increases brightness, while Fit increases contrast
D. Fill deletes the photo
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Fill" expands the image to cover the entire shape container (trimming edges), whereas "Fit" shrinks the image so all pixels remain visible within the boundaries.

---

### 5. Where can Microsoft 365 subscribers access millions of free, curated, high-resolution stock photos and cutout people directly within PowerPoint?
A. File > Options > Add-ins
B. Insert > Pictures > Stock Images
C. View > Master > ClipArt
D. Design > Variants
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The built-in Microsoft 365 "Stock Images" repository provides access to searchable, curated photography, cutout people, icons, and illustrations.
