# Slide Transitions, Effects & Duration Timing

Transitions are the visual animations that occur when your presentation advances from one slide to the next. When applied thoughtfully with appropriate duration and subtle motion, transitions unify your deck into a coherent cinematic story rather than an abrupt sequence of static posters.

![Slide Transitions & Morph](/images/tutorials/ms-powerpoint/transitions-and-morph.svg)

---

## Transitions vs. Animations: The Fundamental Distinction

Beginners often confuse transitions with animations:
- **Slide Transitions**: Animate the **entire slide canvas** as it enters or leaves the screen when moving between slides.
- **Object Animations**: Animate **individual objects** (a specific bullet line, a picture, a chart bar) located *inside* a single slide.

---

## The Transitions Ribbon Gallery

Click the **Transitions** tab to explore three broad categories of transitions:
1. **Subtle Transitions (Professional Standard)**:
   - **Fade**: The timeless corporate favorite; gently dissolves one slide into the next.
   - **Push**: Slides the new slide in from the bottom, top, left, or right, simulating a continuous vertical or horizontal canvas.
   - **Wipe**: Sweeps across the screen cleanly.
   - **Split / Reveal**: Smooth blinds or curtain reveals.
2. **Exciting Transitions**:
   - **Morph**: Seamlessly morphs shared objects from one slide to the next (detailed in the next topic).
   - **Curtains, Wind, Fracture, Crush, Origami**: Highly dramatic 3D physics effects.
   - *Design Note*: Exciting transitions are fun for student projects or creative openers, but avoid them in executive boardroom meetings where they can distract from data.
3. **Dynamic Content Transitions**:
   - Transitions only the content placeholders (text and pictures) while keeping the background canvas locked motionless in place.

---

## Effect Options & Customizing Motion

Most transitions feature custom directions accessible via **Transitions > Effect Options**:
- For **Push**: Choose *From Bottom*, *From Left*, *From Right*, or *From Top*.
- For **Wipe**: Choose *From Top-Right*, *From Bottom-Left*, etc.

---

## Timing, Duration & Automatic Progression

In the **Timing** group on the right side of the Transitions tab:
- **Duration**: Controls how fast the transition plays (e.g., `00.50` seconds for a snappy fade, or `01.50` seconds for a cinematic sweep). Setting duration too high (e.g., 5 seconds) forces the audience to wait uncomfortably.
- **Sound**: Plays an audio effect on slide transition (e.g., *Chime*, *Click*, *Whoosh*). Recommended setting for business: **[No Sound]**.
- **Apply To All**: Instantly copies the selected transition, effect direction, and duration across every slide in your presentation. This is the gold standard for design consistency!
- **Advance Slide Options**:
  - **[✓] On Mouse Click**: The slide advances only when you click the mouse, press the spacebar, or hit the right arrow key (standard for live human delivery).
  - **[✓] After: [00:10.00]**: The slide advances automatically after a set duration. Essential for self-running trade show kiosks, digital signage, and automated photo reels.

# Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. What is the fundamental difference between a slide transition and an object animation in PowerPoint?
A. Transitions are for text only; animations are for videos
B. A transition animates the change from one slide to the next, while an animation moves an individual object on a single slide
C. Transitions only work on Macs; animations only work on Windows
D. Transitions require an internet connection
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Transitions control the movement between whole slides, whereas animations control the behavior of distinct objects situated within a single slide.

---

### 2. Which subtle transition is considered the corporate gold standard for modern, distraction-free business presentations?
A. Fracture
B. Airplane
C. Fade
D. Origami
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The simple "Fade" transition provides an elegant, distraction-free visual bridge between slides suitable for corporate environments.

---

### 3. Which button applies your chosen transition style, effect direction, and duration to every slide in the deck simultaneously?
A. Sync Slides
B. Apply To All
C. Master Transition
D. Duplicate All
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Apply To All" propagates the active transition settings across all slides in the deck, ensuring unified visual flow.

---

### 4. What happens if you uncheck "On Mouse Click" and configure "After: 00:05.00" in the Advance Slide settings?
A. The presentation is password locked
B. The slide advances automatically after exactly 5 seconds without requiring human input
C. The slide is deleted after 5 seconds
D. The slide pauses for 5 minutes
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Unchecking "On Mouse Click" and configuring an "After" time creates an automated kiosk presentation that advances slides on a precise timer.

---

### 5. What setting controls how quickly or slowly a transition animation plays out?
A. Delay
B. Duration
C. Trigger
D. Frame Rate
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Duration" specifies the exact time (in seconds and fractions of a second) taken for the transition animation to complete.
